The systems I run on myself

Governed autonomy, visible to the owner.

The Orrery organizes measurable work across a specialized digital workforce. The Armillary makes that work, its evidence, its limits, and its approval points visible in one command console.

Platform, console, builder

Three systems, one operating discipline.

I run this architecture on my own business. It can organize work, surface failures, preserve lessons, and prepare reviewed software without granting an agent authority it has not earned.

Operations platform

The Orrery

The Orrery turns business goals into organized, measurable work. It can identify priorities, create work orders, assign specialists, monitor progress, verify evidence, record lessons, and report decisions to the owner.

Command console

The Armillary

The Armillary is the Orrery's central command console: one place to see what is queued, active, blocked, completed, or verified, what the workforce learned, and where human approval is required.

Local application builder

Personal App Engine

A single-user tool I built and use. A plain-language request becomes a real Next.js project inside a zero-egress Docker sandbox, then pauses for human review before anything is released.

Measured proof

The boundaries are implemented, not decorative.

Capability describes what the architecture can support. These measured facts show how I govern the system I run on my own work.

12 agent definitionsThree standing leadership personas, one read-only overseer, and eight crew manifests with explicit tool allowlists.
16-stage nightly chainRuns on a schedule and produces a machine-written completion record and morning brief.
Weekly synthesisA read-only overseer compares report streams across domains on a weekly cadence.
More than 900 citations verifiedA separate program opens the cited file and checks the named line. One bad citation rejects the entire report; at least nine reports have been rejected.
More than 60 honesty adjudicationsA second, independent agent grades the first agent's honesty on an earned-trust ladder.
At least 90 proposalsAgent proposals produced at least six promotions, and every promotion required human approval.
Default-deny fleet policyBudgets, permissions, protected files, and operating boundaries are enforced centrally; overnight actions include a deterministic written undo command.
More than 470 App Engine testsZero-egress sandboxing, encrypted secrets, default-deny commands, and structural refusal of automatic money, command, and secret use.

The through-line: the system can look, compare, check, draft, and propose. It cannot spend, publish, or release sensitive work on its own. The owner controls strategy, spending, protected information, and final release.
